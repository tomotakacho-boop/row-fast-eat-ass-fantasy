Power Rankings model

One forward-looking score. One earned score.

Week 0 is a roster forecast. Once games begin, actual performance earns an increasing share of the published Power Score. This tab separates those ideas so draft reputation never masquerades as weekly production.

The two inputs

What every 0–100 rating means

01

Team Strength

The post-draft roster grade and forward-looking outlook. It estimates how strong a team should be from this point forward, independent of its record.

  • 45% projected starting-lineup production
  • 20% value over replacement
  • 15% weekly floor
  • 10% ceiling and spike-week potential
  • 10% depth, positional coverage, and resilience
02

Performance Index

The backward-looking grade earned on the field. It rewards strong scoring and good decisions without giving a team full credit or blame for opponent luck.

  • 40% all-play performance
  • 25% points scored versus projection
  • 20% lineup efficiency
  • 15% opponent-adjusted result

Published score

League-relative normalization

75 + (10 × league z-score)

Each underlying composite is standardized against the twelve teams, then bounded from 40 to 99. A 75 is league-average context—not a school grade.

Editorial layer

The model ranks; people explain

Records, player highlights, shortfalls, matchup context, and weekly blurbs explain the numerical result. Commentary does not secretly change the score.

Guardrails

Small samples stay small

Opponent difficulty is adjusted, bench points are not treated as starter output, inactive players are excluded, and no single game can erase the roster baseline overnight.

Sample status

The Week 0–3 archive is an interface and calibration mockup. Its records, scores, player performances, and blurbs are illustrative—not live ESPN results. The same layout can accept real weekly inputs after games are played.
